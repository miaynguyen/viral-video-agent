# Video advice

## Video 1

```
Video posted 2026-05-07 17:17:00
Prediction: Likely not viral (29% chance of 100k+ views)
Average watch time: 16.2 s (73% of the viral level)
Completion rate: 24% (68% of the viral level)
Share rate: 0.34% (76% of the viral level)
Weakest metric: Completion rate
Strongest metric: Share rate
About the video:
Hook (first line said or shown): Wait for the chorus
Video type: teaser
Song or topic: new single - chorus
Goal of the post: drive pre-saves
```

**What's working:** Share rate is your strongest metric (76% of the viral level), so the chorus teaser already gives people something to send.

**Weakest metric: completion (24%, 68% of viral).** Your hook promises the chorus, so viewers likely leave if it arrives late or the clip runs long.

**Tactics to test:**
1. **Reach the chorus sooner.** Trim the intro so the chorus starts within the first few seconds after "Wait for the chorus." Keep only the chorus and the line that sets it up.
2. **End right after the chorus.** Cut any outro and finish on the chorus hit. Add a short on-screen pre-save prompt at that moment, when attention is highest, instead of asking viewers to watch to the end.

**Avoid:** "watch until the end" prompts. Meta treats them as engagement bait.

```
Hook (first line said or shown): I wrote this song in 10 minutes
Video type: behind the scenes
Song or topic: new single - writing process
Goal of the post: grow followers
```

Strongest metric: average watch time (108% of viral). Viewers stay through the behind-the-scenes process, so the pacing is working.

Share rate is the gap (71% of viral, 0.32% vs. about 0.45%). To lift it on your next writing-process video, try these:

1. **Add a high-arousal moment.** Your hook, "I wrote this song in 10 minutes," is a surprise. Make the reveal more striking, such as a surprising twist mid-process or a dramatic "it all clicked" moment. Positive surprise and amusement are more shareable than calm, process-only footage.
2. **Give viewers a takeaway to pass on.** For a songwriting process, end with one practical tip, like a method for writing fast ("start with the chorus hook, not the verse"). Someone who writes music might send it to a friend who does too.

Keep the hook and the pacing as they are, since those are already working.

# Short-Form Video Playbook

Tactics for improving the three metrics the viral prediction model uses. The agent picks the section that matches a video's weakest metric. Every tactic is a hypothesis to test on the next video, not a guarantee.

## Why these three metrics matter

Meta's own explanation of Facebook Reels ranking says it predicts how long a viewer will watch a reel, using signals like how many times others have watched it to completion. It also predicts whether a viewer will share, like or comment. Watch time, completion and shares are therefore direct inputs to distribution, not just vanity numbers.
Source: Meta Transparency Center, "Facebook Reels AI system" (https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-reels/)

In this account's data (649 Facebook Reels), completion rate was the strongest single predictor of passing 100k views, followed by average watch time, then share rate. Like rate barely mattered.

---

## 1. Average watch time (viral benchmark: about 22 seconds)

**What a low number means:** viewers leave early, usually in the first few seconds. The hook did not earn attention.

**Tactics**
1. **Rework the first 3 seconds.** Meta's creator guidance says the first few seconds decide whether viewers keep watching, and recommends checking the retention curve to see where people drop off. Start on the most interesting moment, not an introduction.
2. **Put a person speaking on screen right away.** An Emplifi analysis of Facebook Reels found that videos with human speech in the first 3 seconds had about 25% higher 10-second retention than music-only videos, and a person on screen in the first 3 seconds lifted 10-second retention by about 10%.
3. **Open a question the video answers later.** Lead with a specific promise or problem ("the one mistake that…") and deliver the payoff near the end, so viewers have a reason to stay.

**Avoid:** explicit "watch until the end" prompts. Meta lists these as engagement bait unless the payoff is genuinely meaningful.

---

## 2. Completion rate (viral benchmark: about 35%)

**What a low number means:** viewers start watching but quit before the end. The video is too long for its content, or the middle drags.

**Tactics**
1. **Cut the length to fit one idea.** A short video should carry one claim and its payoff. Trim setup, repeated points and slow transitions. If the retention curve slides steadily with no single drop, the fix is tighter pacing.
2. **Move the payoff later and end on it.** A drop halfway through usually means the payoff came too early or the topic changed. End right after the payoff, with no long outro.
3. **Make the ending connect back to the start.** An ending that closes the question from the hook, or loops naturally into the first frame, encourages viewers to finish and rewatch.

---

## 3. Share rate (viral benchmark: about 0.45%, roughly 4–5 shares per 1,000 views)

**What a low number means:** people watch but don't send it to anyone. It lacks an emotional charge or a "this is so you" moment.

**Tactics**
1. **Aim for high-arousal emotion.** Research by Berger and Milkman (Journal of Marketing Research, 2012) found that content evoking high-arousal emotions (awe, amusement, surprise, even anxiety) is shared more than low-arousal content (contentment, sadness). Positive high-arousal content is the safest bet for a brand.
2. **Make it about the viewer's friend.** Frame the content around a relatable situation or a specific type of person ("for anyone who…") so viewers think of someone to send it to.
3. **Give one useful takeaway worth passing on.** A clear, practical tip or surprising fact gives people a reason to share it as helpful information.
